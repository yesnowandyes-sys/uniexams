# Style Guide: ESAT/NSAA Physics P4 (Thermal Physics)

## Question Structure
*   **Short Stem with "Best/Least" Options:** The most common format (e.g., Q4, Q19) presents a table of variables or a list of conditions and asks "In which case is the rate of thermal energy transfer lowest/highest?" This tests proportional reasoning without requiring full calculation of the final value.
*   **Stem + Mini-Scenario:** Questions often describe a physical setup (diver, soldering iron, star) with a specific change (movement, heating, aging) and ask for the resulting state variable.
*   **Two-Step Calculations:** Frequently combines two formulae. For example, Q5 requires calculating Energy Input ($P \times t$) AND Energy Stored ($mc\Delta T$) to find the difference.

## Difficulty Calibration
*   **Easy (Band 1-3):** Direct application of single definitions (e.g., identifying convection currents from a diagram) or simple proportionalities (doubling radius $\rightarrow$ area $\times 4$).
*   **Medium (Band 4-6):** Multi-step energy balance (Calorimetry). Requires identifying Heat Lost = Heat Gained + Latent Heat. Also includes qualitative questions where the "intuitive" answer (based on surface color) is incorrect due to the dominance of temperature ($T^4$).
*   **Hard (Band 7-9):** Compound proportional reasoning (e.g., Q1/Q12 where Radius increases by 100 AND Temp decreases by 2, requiring calculation of $100^2 / 2^4$). Also, questions requiring the derivation of a formula from a graph (Q22) or non-linear volume/depth relationships (Q28).

## Wording Conventions
*   **Specific Heat Capacity ($c$):** Usually denoted in $\text{J kg}^{-1} \text{°C}^{-1}$ (or $\text{J g}^{-1} \text{°C}^{-1}$). Note the units carefully (kg vs g) as this is a common trap.
*   **Power vs. Energy:** "Power radiated" ($P$) vs "Energy transferred" ($E$). Time is always explicitly given if Energy is required.
*   **"Which of the following..." statements:** Often used for conduction insulation questions (Q21), asking to select a combination of valid physical explanations.

## Arithmetic & Calculator-Free Patterns
*   **Clean Constants:** Specific heats are typically 400, 4200 (water), or round numbers. Latent heats are often 300 or 330 (simplified real values).
*   **Factor Manipulation:** Most "comparative" questions rely on factors of 2, 4, 10, 100.
    *   *Pattern:* Area $\times 4$ (diameter doubles), Volume $\times 8$, Area $\times 100$ (radius $\times 10$), Stefan-Boltzmann $2^4 = 16$.
    *   *Calculation Strategy:* Cancel $k$ or constants immediately. Look for the "delta" (difference) in temperatures ($\Delta T$) rather than absolute values.
*   **Volume Ratios:** Gas law questions usually use simple integer ratios (e.g., 90 to 720 $\rightarrow$ factor of 8). Students should recognize factors of 8 ($2^3$) or 27 ($3^3$) quickly.

## Common Visuals
*   **Cross-Sections:** Cylindrical bars (Q4, Q11) showing length $l$ and diameter $d$.
*   **Temperature Gradients:** Graphs of Temperature vs. Distance for composite materials.
*   **Phase Diagrams:** Heating curves (Temperature vs. Time) with flat plateaus.
