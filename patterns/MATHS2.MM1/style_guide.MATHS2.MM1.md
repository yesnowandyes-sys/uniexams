# ESAT MATHS2.MM1 Style Guide

## 1. Question Structure & Patterns
*   **Stem Formats:**
    *   **"Complete set of values":** The dominant format for inequalities and roots (Q1, Q2, Q3, Q19). The phrasing is strict; options are almost always intervals.
    *   **"Which one of the following...":** Used for simplification and algebraic manipulation (Q6, Q9). Options A-G are usually distinct algebraic forms.
    *   **"Consider the following...":** Common in TMUA/NSAA for defining functions or conditions before asking for a derivation (Q23, Q41).
*   **Syntactic Vectors:**
    *   Questions frequently combine a function definition with a transformation ($f(x-a)$, $f(kx)$) or a condition ($f(x) > 0$).
    *   The variable $x$ is standard; parameters are often $a, b, c, k, m, p$.

## 2. Difficulty Calibration
*   **Band 1-3 (Foundation):** Direct application of laws of indices, simplifying rational expressions with factorization (Q6), finding discriminants of quadratics.
*   **Band 4-6 (Core):**
    *   Inequalities involving parameters (finding range of $a$ for distinct roots).
    *   Quadratic inequalities in non-standard forms (e.g., $2x^4 - 9x^2 + 4 > 0$ in Q2).
    *   Binomial expansion coefficients for specific terms.
*   **Band 7-9 (Advanced):**
    *   "Hidden" quadratics (indices/logarithms). E.g., Q7 ($2^x - (\sqrt{2})^{x+6} + 12 = 0$).
    *   Functional relationships and symmetry (Q22, Q29).
    *   Simultaneous equations with non-linear constraints (Q19, Q43).

## 3. Calculator-Free Arithmetic Patterns
*   **Coefficient Selection:**
    *   Coefficients in expansions are chosen to be calculable mentally or with minimal scratchpad work (Pascal's triangle values, small powers).
    *   *Example:* $(1-2x)^5(1+2x)^5$ (Q4) relies on recognizing the difference of squares $(1-4x^2)^5$.
*   **Roots and Solutions:**
    *   Quadratic roots are often integers or simple surds ($\pm\sqrt{2}$).
    *   "Differ by 2/6" problems (Q17, Q11) result in perfect square discriminants.
    *   Constant terms in equations frequently cancel or simplify to 0, 1, or small integers (e.g., Q30 where constant $q$ vanishes in calculation).

## 4. Wording Conventions
*   **"The non-zero constant...":** A standard exclusionary clause to prevent division by zero trivialities (Q20).
*   **"For real x":** Emphasizes the domain, crucial for inequality questions involving even roots or logs.
*   **"Necessary and sufficient":** Standard TMUA/NSAA logic phrasing for condition equivalence.
