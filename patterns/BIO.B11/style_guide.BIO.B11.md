# ESAT BIO.B11 Plant Physiology Style Guide

## Question Structure Patterns
*   **Combined Skills (Graph + Mechanism):** A prevalent pattern involves a graph showing the rate of photosynthesis or transpiration under varying conditions. The student must interpret the shape of the graph (slope, plateau, trough) and select a mechanistic biological explanation (e.g., enzyme denaturation, stomatal closure, limiting factors).
    *   *Examples:* Q1 (Temperature graph), Q13 (Light intensity graph), Q19 (Temperature adaptation).
*   **Apparatus Calculations:** Several questions describe a specific experimental setup (bubble potometer, capillary tube) and require the student to calculate a biological rate (volume per time) from geometric measurements (distance, diameter).
    *   *Examples:* Q3, Q4, Q15, Q22.
*   **Process/Pathway Sequencing:** Questions ask the student to trace the path of a substance (radioactive carbon, water) or identify the correct transport mechanism (translocation vs. transpiration) from source to sink.
    *   *Examples:* Q2 (Radioactive sugars), Q20 (Water potential gradient).

## Difficulty Calibration
*   **Band 1-3 (Foundation):** Recall of definitions (Translocation vs. Transpiration), basic net photosynthesis equation ($Rate = Gross - Respiration$), identifying the products of photosynthesis.
    *   *Evidence:* Q2 (Transport definitions).
*   **Band 4-6 (Application):** Interpreting limiting factors from graphs (identifying which factor is limiting based on the x-axis and curve shape), simple unit conversions for bubble rates ($Volume = \pi r^2 \times distance$).
    *   *Evidence:* Q1 (Limiting factor temp), Q4 (Volume calc).
*   **Band 7-9 (Deep Analysis):** Multi-variable reasoning (e.g.,humidity changes inferred from transpiration rates in Q11), calculating water potential gradients (Q20), understanding the interplay of respiration and photosynthesis to explain zero net gas exchange (Q13).

## Wording Conventions
*   **Qualifiers:** Frequent use of "Which of the following conclusions is/are correct?" indicating a multiple-response format common in Section 1 of the NSAA/ESAT.
*   **Variables:** Questions consistently specify "All other variables were kept constant."
*   **Terminology:** Specific terms are used strictly:
    *   "Net rate" vs "Gross rate".
    *   "Water potential" (implied solutes/pressure).
    *   "Translocation" (sugars/phloem) vs "Transpiration" (water/xylem).

## Calculator-Free Arithmetic Patterns
*   **Geometry:** Calculations rely on the cylinder volume formula $V = \pi r^2 h$.
    *   *Pattern:* Often $d$ (diameter) is given as a simple integer (e.g., 1mm or 2mm), so $r = 0.5$. The constant $\pi$ is left in the answer (e.g., $0.5\pi$, $4\pi$).
    *   *Avoids:* Complex decimals; students handle integers and simple fractions (0.5) and leave $\pi$ symbolic.
*   **Unit Conversions:** Conversions are powers of 10 (mm to m, cm to m, $\mu m$ to mm).
    *   *Example:* Q22 requires converting 60$\mu m$ to mm or cm for volume.
*   **Rates:** Inverse square law ($I \propto 1/d^2$) is applied conceptually or with simple integer squares (e.g., $10^2 = 100$).
    *   *Evidence:* Q18 implies the relationship but often tests the concept of the curve rather than precise calculation in recent years.
