# ESAT Topic C6 Style Guide: Chemical Bonding, Structure and Properties

## 1. Question Architecture & Cognitive Load
*   **Multi-stage reasoning (S2):** Advanced questions (Band 7-9) typically combine data analysis (mass calculations) with structural theory.
    *   *Example Path:* Calculate empirical formula -> Determine Lewis structure -> Apply VSEPR -> Predict angle (Q14, Q15).
*   **Data Interpretation (S1):** Mid-band questions often provide a table of physical properties (mp, bp, conductivity) and require the identification of bonding type or state of matter (Q1, Q7).
*   **"Which is NOT" logic:** Negative logic is frequently used to test lattice geometry (Q11) or reaction types (Q2), increasing cognitive load by requiring the rejection of three valid scenarios to find the invalid one.

## 2. Wording & Stem Conventions
*   **Qualifiers:** "Simple molecular," "Giant covalent," "Giant ionic," "Simple oxide."
*   **State Specification:** Stems explicitly state "gaseous state," "molten," or "aqueous solution" to define conditions for conductivity/bonding context.
*   **Stem Complexity:**
    *   S1 Stems: 1-2 sentences, often defining a specific element X or a set of properties.
    *   S2 Stems: 3-4 sentences, providing contextual data (e.g., "A lattice can be thought of as a stack of layered planes...") or experimental parameters (masses/temperatures).

## 3. Visual & Quantitative Patterns
*   **Diagrammatic Reasoning:** Questions involving crystal lattices use abstract 2D representations of 3D structures (circles/shading). Students must mentally rotate or stack these layers.
*   **Calculator-Free Math:**
    *   **Ratios:** Simple integer ratios (1:2, 3:2) are common for formula determination.
    *   **VSEPR Math:** Involves manipulating bond lengths ($L$) and atomic distances ($d$). Students are not required to solve the triangle, but to *select* the correct trigonometric expression.
    *   *Pattern:* Options will be `sin^-1(x/y)` vs `cos^-1(x/y)`. The values are often decimals (e.g., 0.1205) derived from halving distances.
*   **Periodic Table Props:** Small periodic table grids are provided for specific questions (Q6) to test localized trend spotting.

## 4. Difficulty Calibration
*   **Band 4-6:** Identification of bond types (ionic/covalent/metallic) based on physical property tables. Basic VSEPR shapes (linear, trigonal planar) for simple molecules.
*   **Band 7-9:**
    *   **Lattice Visualization:** Determining valid stacking planes in ionic crystals.
    *   **Hybridization/Distortion:** Predicting bond angles in ions or molecules with lone pairs where the angle deviates from the ideal (e.g., < 109.5° or < 90° in see-saw shapes).
    *   **Enthalpy vs. Temperature:** Distinguishing concepts of bond strength vs. intermolecular forces in boiling point explanations.
