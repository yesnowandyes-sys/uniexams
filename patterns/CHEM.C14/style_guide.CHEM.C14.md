### Topic: Metals (CHEM.C14)

**Question Structure Patterns**
*   **Logical Deduction Grid:** Questions in this topic almost always present a set of 3-4 discrete statements (facts) about 4 unknown metals (P, Q, R, S or M, Q, R, T).
*   **Evidence Types:**
    *   *Extraction Method:* "Extracted by electrolysis" vs "Extracted by carbon (reduction)".
    *   *Displacement:* "Metal X displaces Metal Y from solution" or "Metal X reacts with oxide of Metal Y".
    *   *Acid Reaction:* Reaction vs No reaction with HCl (to benchmark relative to Hydrogen).
*   **Target:** The standard output is a ranking order (Most Reactive to Least Reactive).

**Difficulty Calibration**
*   **Foundational (Band 1-3):** Direct application of the Reactivity Series. E.g., "Here is a list of metals. Which is most reactive?" (Not observed in corpus, but implied by spec).
*   **Core (Band 4-6):** (Corpus Observed) Multi-step logical inference. The student must convert "Extraction by Carbon" to "Medium Reactivity" and "Displaces Y" to "X > Y", then synthesize these into a single linear sequence.
*   **Advanced (Band 7-9):** (Potential) Quantitative electrochemistry (though usually separate C18 topic) or complex transition metal chemistry (catalysis, variable oxidation states) which appears in the spec text but not the provided questions.

**Wording Conventions**
*   **Variable Naming:** Uses capitalized single-letter labels (P, Q, R, S). This abstracts the problem, preventing students from relying on memorized series (like "Mg is above Zn") without applying the logic.
*   **Phrasing:**
    *   "Forms positive ions more readily" = Higher reactivity.
    *   "Extracted by reaction with carbon" = Below Carbon (but above Hydrogen effectively, or at least distinct from the electrolysis group).
    *   "Displaces" = The solid metal is more reactive than the metal in the solution.

**Calculator-Free Arithmetic Patterns**
*   **Arithmetic:** None required. This is purely a logic/verbal reasoning task within a science context.
*   **Logic Load:** Students typically need to hold 2-3 relationships in working memory simultaneously (e.g., "R > Q", "S > P", "S/P are high reactivity", "Q/R are medium reactivity").

**Key Conceptual Hooks**
*   **The Carbon Boundary:** The most critical 'step' in these questions is categorizing metals into "Above Carbon" (Electrolysis) and "Below Carbon" (Reduction).
*   **The Hydrogen Boundary:** Used to split the "Below Carbon" group into "Reacts with Acid" and "Does not react with Acid".
