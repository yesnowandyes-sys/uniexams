# ESAT Topic BIO.B1 Cells — Style Guide

## 1. Question Structure & Formatting
*   **Format:** Questions are overwhelmingly multiple-choice (MCQ).
*   **Stems:** Often begin with "Which of the following statements is/are correct?" or "Which one of the following comparisons is correct?".
*   **Numbered Statements:** A common format (observed in ~60% of corpus) presents 3 numbered premises/statements, then asks to identify the combination (e.g., "A: 1 only", "B: 1 and 2 only", "H: 1, 2 and 3"). This tests the ability to evaluate multiple independent facts.
*   **Data/Tables:** Section 2 (Advanced) questions frequently utilize tables with missing rows or multiple columns to compare (e.g., Q21, Q22).

## 2. Difficulty Calibration
*   **Band 1-3 (Recall):** Direct identification of organelles in specific cell types (e.g., "Which cell has no mitochondria?").
*   **Band 4-6 (Application/Logic):**
    *   **Genomics:** Distinguishing between the *presence* of a gene, *expression* of a protein, and the *alleles* present in different cell types (Cheek vs Sperm vs Stem).
    *   **Microscopy Math:** Calculating magnification ($Drawing \div Actual$) involving unit conversions ($\mu m \to mm$).
*   **Band 7-9 (Quantitative/Deep Insight):**
    *   **Respirometry/Enzyme Kinetics:** Calculating enzyme mass given density and volume, or inferring cellular respiration rates from mitochondrial volume percentages (Q17, Q18).
    *   **Dilution Calculations:** Back-calculating original cell concentration from diluted hemocytometer counts (Q19).

## 3. Wording Conventions
*   **"Healthy human":** This qualifier is standard in questions regarding genome/cell types to rule out pathological states (e.g., cancer, anemia) unless specified.
*   **"Assume no mutations":** Used to simplify questions about genetic variation (Q4, Q14) so students focus on Mendelian meiosis/mitosis rules rather than mutation sources.
*   **"Mature red blood cell":** The standard trick term for "no nucleus" and "no mitochondria".
*   **Units:** Strict use of SI units. Note: Questions often mix $\mu m$, $mm$, and $cm$ to test conversion skills.

## 4. Calculator-Free Arithmetic Patterns
*   **Standard Form:** Almost all numerical answers are in Standard Form ($A \times 10^n$).
*   **Geometry:** Simple sphere volume ($V = \frac{4}{3}\pi r^3$) and area ($A = 4\pi r^2$) formulas are provided in the question stem (Q17).
*   **Magnification Calculation:**
    *   Pattern: $Image_{size} \div Real_{size}$.
    *   Trap: $Image$ in $cm/mm$, $Real$ in $\mu m$.
    *   Example: $5.0 \, cm \div 12.5 \, \mu m = 50,000 \, \mu m \div 12.5 \, \mu m = 4,000$.
*   **Indices/Doubling:** Bacterial division questions involve powers of 2 (e.g., doubling every 20 mins $\to n$ divisions in 24 hours).
*   **Percentage Volume:** "Account for 12% of the volume" $\to$ Requires finding $12\%$ of $V_{sphere}$.
