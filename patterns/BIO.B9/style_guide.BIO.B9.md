# ESAT Biology B9 (Animal Physiology) Style Guide

## 1. Question Formats & Structure
*   **Data-Heavy Contexts:** Questions frequently rely on interpreting **graphs (ECG, Volume/Time, Concentration gradients)** or **tables** of chemical concentrations.
    *   *Example:* Q4, Q11 require correlating a vertical drop in a volume graph with specific valve closures.
    *   *Example:* Q8 provides a complex table of glucose/oxygen/urea levels entering/leaving organs to deduce organ identity.
*   **Diagram Labeling:** Anatomy questions utilize simplified diagrams of the heart, kidney, or nervous systems. They rarely ask for rote memorization of labels; instead, they ask for functional identification (e.g., "Which vessel has the highest concentration of urea?" Q2).
*   **Multi-Step Quantitative Biology:** S2 questions (harder) frequently embed math.
    *   *Volume of cylinder:* $V = \pi r^2 h$ (Q24, Q27, Q34).
    *   *Rate of flow:* Volume / Time.
    *   *Percentage changes:* Comparing concentrations based on fixed mass vs changing volume (Q28).

## 2. Calculator-Free Arithmetic Patterns
Since calculators are not allowed, numbers are designed to be manipulable, or "long" numbers are used to test estimation/scientific notation skills.

*   **Geometry focus:**
    *   Calculations often involve $\pi$ or $r^2$.
    *   *Pattern:* Radius is often a "nice" number (e.g., 2mm, 2.8mm), or the answer is left in terms of $\pi$ (e.g., "50 × (1.4)² × π", Q27).
    *   *Trap:* Using diameter instead of radius.
*   **Scientific Notation:**
    *   Used for cell counts or molecule counts (Q13, Q32).
    *   *Calculation:* $4 \times 10^6 \text{ cells} / \text{mm}^3 \times 6000 \text{ mm}^3$.
*   **"Friendly" Percentages:**
    *   Changes are often 20%, 50%, or "decreased by a third/sixth".
    *   *Q23:* "Decreased by a sixth" implies multiplying by 5/6.
    *   *Q31:* 233% increase (implies x3.33).

## 3. Wording Conventions
*   **"Healthy Individual":** A standard qualifier (Q2, Q15, Q22). Students must ignore pathologies unless specified.
*   **"Row Correctly Identifies...":** Used for table-based questions matching structures to functions or concentrations.
*   **"Which of the following is/are correct?":** Almost always used for sets of 3 statements (A-H options). This forces the student to evaluate three distinct mini-scenarios.
*   **Arbitrary Units:** Frequently used for graphs where absolute values don't matter, only the trend (e.g., Q7, Q18, Q33).

## 4. Difficulty Calibration
*   **Band 1-3 (Direct Knowledge):** Basic definitions (e.g., "What is a reflex arc?", Q16), simple word equations for respiration (Q1).
*   **Band 4-6 (Application & Interpretation):** Interpreting a graph to determine the phase of the cardiac cycle (Q4), linking oxygen debt to post-exercise recovery (Q6), understanding negative feedback loops (Q17).
*   **Band 7-9 (Synthesis & Quantitative):**
    *   Two-step physics/bio calculations (e.g., calculating fluid rate from volume change in a capillary tube Q9).
    *   Complex system analysis (e.g., identifying the effect of an inhibitor on sodium ion removal rates Q35, or determining water balance shifts Q28).
