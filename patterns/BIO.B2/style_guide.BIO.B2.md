### Topic: BIO.B2 Movement across Membranes — Style Guide

**1. Question Structure & Cognitive Load**
*   **Data Interpretation Focus:** A significant portion of questions (approx. 60%) present visual data (U-tubes, osmometer graphs, potato mass tables) or numerical tables (ion concentrations). Success requires extracting trends (e.g., "percentage change in mass") or calculating gradients (Q9).
*   **"Which Statement is Correct" Format:** Multi-statement questions ("1, 2, and 3") are prevalent (e.g., Q2, Q7, Q14). This increases difficulty; the student must evaluate three distinct concepts (e.g., active transport, osmosis, diffusion) simultaneously rather than picking a single fact.
*   **Experimental Scenarios:** Questions rarely ask for a definition. Instead, they describe a specific setup (e.g., "inhibiting respiration," "sliding membrane," "nanomachines") and ask for the outcome. This tests the *application* of the mechanism to a novel context.

**2. Wording Conventions**
*   **Precision in Terminology:** The spec and questions strictly distinguish "substance," "solute," and "water." Questions often trap students who loosely use "diffusion" when "osmosis" is required for water.
*   **Qualitative Descriptors:** Phrases like "very dilute glucose solution," "concentrated sugar solution," and "net movement" are standard. "Haemolysis" and "Plasmolysis" are the key biological outcomes for animal and plant cells respectively.
*   **"Net Movement":** This is the critical phrase. Questions often ask about the *net* flow (Q3, Q4), requiring the student to weigh random molecular motion against the concentration gradient.

**3. Calculator-Free Arithmetic Patterns**
*   **Concentration Conversions:** Frequent requirement to convert between mmol dm⁻³ and $\mu$mol dm⁻³ (factor of 1000), or molarity and percentage.
    *   *Example:* Q8 (4 mmol dm⁻³ vs 4000 $\mu$mol dm⁻³).
*   **Percentage Calculations:** Expect calculations of percentage change (Start vs. End mass) and "percentage of X that is Y".
    *   *Example:* Q22 (Volume of dyed region vs Total volume of a cube). Calculation: $(3^3 - 1.5^3) / 3^3$.
*   **Surface Area / Volume Ratios:** While usually conceptual, S2 questions (Q16, Q22) involve ratios. If calculated, numbers are kept simple (e.g., comparing 1cm vs 3cm cubes results in clear factors of 3 or 9).

**4. Difficulty Calibration**
*   **Band 1-3:** Definitions of osmosis/diffusion; simple "high to low" movement; identifying hypertonic/hypotonic scenarios.
*   **Band 4-6:** Interpreting multi-variable graphs; U-tube logic; distinguishing active transport from passive transport based on energy/respiration cues; unit conversions.
*   **Band 7-9:** Complex reasoning involving membrane permeability changes (cholesterol effects, Q21); calculating water potential changes from % mass data in tables (Q18); integrating SA:Vol ratios with diffusion rates (Q16).
