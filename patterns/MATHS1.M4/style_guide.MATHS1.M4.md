# Style Guide — MATHS1.M4 (Algebra)

- Module: Mathematics 1 (M1)
- Corpus questions classified under this topic: 155
- corpus_backed: True

---

## Question Structure Patterns

**Format Distribution:**
*   **Multiple Choice (A-H):** Every question in the corpus is Multiple Choice with 5-8 options.
*   **Expression Equality:** "Which one of the following is a simplification of..." or "Which of the following is equal to..." is the dominant phrasing for algebraic manipulation tasks (Q2, Q6, Q13, Q27).
*   **Value Calculation:** "What is the value of [variable]?" or "What is the length of..." follows setup contexts (Q3, Q7, Q28).
*   **Set/Interval Notation:** Inequalities often ask for the "complete set of solutions" or "complete set of values" (Q1, Q10, Q32, Q53). Correct options utilize strict inequalities ($<$, $>$) or intervals, while distractors often test boundary inclusion/exclusion.

## Difficulty Calibration

**Band 4-6 (Foundation Algebra):**
*   Manipulating single-variable linear inequalities (Q1, Q47).
*   Direct application of Laws of Indices with positive integers (Q14).
*   Basic substitution into linear formulae (Q33, Q12).
*   Expanding squares of simple binomials $(x+a)^2$.

**Band 7-9 (Complex Manipulation):**
*   **Rational Algebra:** Simplifying fractions involving quadratic polynomials, difference of squares factorization required before cancellation (Q6, Q13, Q27).
*   **Rearrangement:** Making a variable the subject when it appears in denominators and quadratic terms (Q4, Q11, Q50, Q54). Often requires two distinct algebraic moves (e.g., cross-multiplying, then square rooting).
*   **Surds:** Manipulating expressions with nested or combined roots, often requiring rationalization or recognizing patterns like $(\sqrt{a}+\sqrt{b})^2$ (Q15, Q24, Q52).
*   **Hidden Quadratics:** Problems that appear linear but require quadratic solutions (e.g., $\sin^2$ substitutions or exponential equations with $a^x$ treated as $y$) (Q21, Q44).

## Wording Conventions

*   **Implicit positivity:** Questions often imply or state "positive values" to avoid absolute value ambiguity in roots (Q41).
*   **"Simplification" Defined:** "Simplify fully" is a standard instruction implying all like terms must be collected, fractions reduced, and factorizations canceled where valid.
*   **"Make... the subject":** Standard phrasing for rearrangement. The target variable is usually the one buried deepest in the expression (e.g., inside a fraction and a square root in Q50).
*   **Simultaneous Contexts:** Instead of "Solve the simultaneous equations," questions are often framed as "Apples cost x, Pears cost y..." (Q12, Q33).

## Calculator-Free Arithmetic Patterns

**Integer Preserving Design:**
Despite forbidding calculators, questions involving complex surds or irrational constants (like $\pi$) are designed to yield clean answers or specific forms.
*   **Surds:** Results often simplify to $a\sqrt{b}$ or $a + b\sqrt{c}$ where $a,b,c$ are small integers (Q2, Q49).
*   **Discriminants:** Quadratics resulting from geometry problems typically have perfect square discriminants to allow for factorable solutions (e.g., Q3, Q28).
*   **Indices:** Powers are chosen so terms cancel out or result in $x^0 = 1$. For example, in Q41, the powers of $y$ sum to zero, allowing $y$ to disappear from the simplified expression.
*   **Fractional Coefficients:** When answers involve fractions, they often have denominators that divide evenly or simplify to integers in the final step (e.g., Q35 involves a substitution that clears the denominator).
