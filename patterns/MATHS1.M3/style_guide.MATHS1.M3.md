# Style Guide for MATHS1.M3: Ratio and Proportion

## Question Structure Patterns
*   **Multi-variable Abstraction:** Questions frequently define variables ($x, y, z$) or physical quantities ($P, Q, R$) with abstract proportional relationships rather than concrete numerical values immediately (e.g., Q4, Q20).
*   **Contextual Embedding:** Pure ratio problems are often wrapped in real-world contexts to test translation of word problems into algebraic expressions. Common contexts include:
    *   **Mixtures/Density:** Alloys, paints, or solutions with different densities/concentrations (Q3, Q24).
    *   **Geometry/Similarity:** Scale models, similar solids, area/volume scaling (Q8, Q9, Q17, Q44).
    *   **Economics/Percentage:** Salary changes, price markups/markdowns, tax calculations (Q13, Q14, Q16, Q37).

## Difficulty Calibration
*   **Easy (Band 1-3):** Direct percentage changes, simple two-part ratio combination, direct proportionality with one step. Arithmetic is usually simple integer manipulation.
*   **Medium (Band 4-6):** Compound percentage changes, indirect proportionality ($1/x$ or $1/\sqrt{x}$), combining ratios where the common term needs scaling (e.g., 5:2 and 3:10), density/mass conversions.
*   **Hard (Band 7-9):** Chains of proportionalities (3+ variables), geometric similarity involving area/volume differences (algebraic manipulation of scale factors), inverse proportionality with percentage changes (e.g., "700% more" implies $8x$, requiring $\sqrt[3]{8}$), problems requiring the formulation of algebraic fractions.

## Wording Conventions
*   **"Simplest form":** Used explicitly for ratio questions (Q1). If the answer isn't simplified, it's likely a distractor or incorrect.
*   **"Resulting percentage change":** Requires determining the direction (increase/decrease) and magnitude. The order of operations in word problems is critical (e.g., "reduced by 25%" vs "increasing the marked price by 25%" - Q37).
*   **"Mathematically similar":** A trigger for scale factor rules ($k, k^2, k^3$).
*   **Density phrasing:** Often uses "Percentage of X is Y" in mixture problems, requiring $Mass = Volume \times Density$ logic.

## Calculator-Free Arithmetic Patterns
*   **Fraction Handling:** Answers are often expressed as fractions or exact values (e.g., $\frac{81}{16}$ in Q2, or fractional ratios).
*   **Integer Constraint:** While inputs can be decimals (0.8), the final options are usually clean integers or simple fractions.
*   **Cube Roots:** Inverse proportionality involving squares or cubes often results in or requires cube roots of integers (e.g., $\sqrt[3]{7}$ or $\sqrt[3]{8}=2$).
*   **Cancelling:** In geometry problems (cylinders/spheres), $\pi$ often cancels out, leaving integer coefficients or simple factors (e.g., Q7, Q44).
