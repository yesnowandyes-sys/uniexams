## Unit Conversion Question Structure

### Arithmetic & Calculation Patterns
*   **Calculation-Free Arithmetic:** Questions in this domain appear designed to be solved without calculators, relying on integer arithmetic or powers of 10.
    *   *Example:* Dividing $200$ by $5$ (Q2) or dividing $30$ by $25$ (implied volume calc in Q1).
    *   *Pattern:* Numerators and denominators are often multiples of 5 or 10, facilitating mental division.

### Wording & Convention
*   **"Approximate" Usage:** In questions involving small physical scales (e.g., Q3), the word "approximate" signals that exact geometric precision is secondary to order-of-magnitude unit conversion.
*   **Mixed Units in Prompt:** Questions frequently provide data in SI units (Newtons, meters) but require the answer in non-SI or CGS derived units (grams, $cm^{-3}$).
*   **Exponential Notation:** Small scales ($10^{-15}$) are written explicitly. Students must be comfortable converting between decimal notation ($0.05$ nm) and standard form ($5 \times 10^{-11}$ m).

### Visual & Contextual Integration
*   **Geometric-Physical Hybrid:** Unit conversion is often the final step in a multi-stage physics problem (e.g., Density = Mass / Volume).
    *   *Workflow:* Extract dimensions $\to$ Calculate Volume ($cm^3$ or $m^3$) $\to$ Apply Formula $\to$ Convert Units.
    *   *Diagram Role:* Diagrams provide geometric constraints (e.g., hole cross-section) but do not indicate the target unit system.

### Difficulty Calibration
*   **Band 1-3 (Easy):** Simple conversion between common units (e.g., km to m).
*   **Band 4-6 (Medium):** Compound unit conversions involving squared/cubed dimensions (e.g., $m^3$ to $cm^3$) or distinguishing units in algebraic fractions (Q2).
*   **Band 7-9 (Hard):** Multi-scale jumps (nanometres to femtometres) or high-stakes single-step conversions where one arithmetic error leads to a listed distractor (Q1, Q3).
