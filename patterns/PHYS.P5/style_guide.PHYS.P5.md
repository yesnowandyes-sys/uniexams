# PHYS.P5 Matter Style Guide

## Arithmetic & Quantitative Constraints
*   **Calculator-Free Arithmetic:**
    *   Calculations rarely exceed 2-3 steps.
    *   **Powers of 10:** Answers frequently involve powers of 10, requiring students to handle indices mentally (e.g., calculating $ 10^{-6} / 10^{-4} $).
    *   **Canceling Zeros:** In questions involving cylinders/pistons (e.g., Q3), dimensions often yield simple ratios (e.g., $ 2.0 \times 10^{-4} / 0.02 $) or involve round numbers (50 kg, 10 N/kg).
    *   **Fractions:** Geometric calculations (Q4, Q10) rely on simple fractions (1/2, 1/3) derived from cross-sections.
    *   **Unit Conversion:** Expect one non-standard unit conversion (e.g., cm$^3$ to m$^3$, g to kg).

## Question Structures
*   **Format:** 90% of questions are Multiple Choice with 8 options (A-H).
*   **Contexts:**
    1.  **Thermal/Fluid Properties:** Questions about convection (Q1/Q6), mixing fluids (Q7), or compressing gases (Q3). These often link *Density* to *Temperature* or *Pressure* via the Ideal Gas law or thermal expansion.
    2.  **Solid Mechanics (Young's Modulus):** A heavy focus on wires and blocks (Q2, Q8, Q11, Q12, Q13, Q14). These typically test:
        *   $ E = \frac{\text{stress}}{\text{strain}} $
        *   $ Energy = \frac{1}{2} F x $
        *   Geometric derivations of Area or Volume.
*   **Data Presentation:** Graphs are used for Hooke's Law (Q13) and abstract variable relationships (Q12). Diagrams are essential for pressure (Q10) and density (Q4).

## Difficulty Calibration
*   **Low Band (Easy):** Direct application of definitions. E.g., "Which statements explain convection?" (Q1), or finding mass by difference (Q7).
*   **Mid Band (Medium):** Multi-step calculations involving Unit Conversions. E.g., Density of a block with a hole (Q4) or Gas Law piston compression (Q3).
*   **High Band (Hard):** Abstract derivation or complex mechanics. E.g., Young's Modulus from a suspended rope midpoint displacement (Q14) or deriving the $ x^2 $ relationship for extension vs unstretched length (Q12).
