# Style Guide: ESAT MATHS1.M2 (Number)

## 1. Question Structure & Format
*   **Format:** Multiple Choice (typically 5-8 options, A-H).
*   **Contexts:** Questions bridge abstract arithmetic and real-world physics/biology applications.
    *   *Pure:* Fraction simplification, surd manipulation, divisibility proofs.
    *   *Applied:* Drug dosages, density of atoms/cubes, depreciation, profit margins, journey costs.
*   **Variable Density:** High. Questions often introduce 3-4 variables (e.g., $m, x, f, p$ in Q4) and require the student to construct a single complex expression from them.

## 2. Calculator-Free Arithmetic Patterns
Since calculators are not allowed, questions are designed to be solved via **cancellation, factoring, and estimation**:
*   **Surd Tricks:** Frequent use of conjugates $(\sqrt{A}+\sqrt{B})(\sqrt{A}-\sqrt{B}) = A-B$ to simplify division (e.g., Q2, Q5).
    *   *Pattern:* Telescoping sums involving surds (Q5) where terms cancel out leaving a simple first/last term remainder.
*   **Prime Factorization:** Large numbers are almost always products of small primes (2, 3, 5).
    *   *Pattern:* Instead of $125$, expect $5^3$. Instead of $81$, expect $3^4$. This allows algebraic cancellation rather than decimal multiplication.
*   **Standard Form:** Physics contexts (Q8, Q11, Q18) use numbers like $10^{26}$ or $10^4$. The arithmetic usually involves adding/subtracting exponents rather than calculating mantissas.

## 3. Wording Conventions & Traps
*   **Ambiguity in "Increase/Decrease":** Phrases like "increased by 125%" (multiply by 2.25) vs "decreased by 40%" (multiply by 0.6) are standard (Q3).
    *   *Trap:* Assuming these operations are commutative or linear (e.g., $+125\% - 40\% \neq +85\%$).
*   **"Which of the following is NOT true?":** Common in Logic/Number theory questions (TMUA sourced). Requires checking every option.
*   **"Correct Expression":** Often asks for the *formula* rather than the value (Q4, Q7).
    *   *Requirement:* High fluency in unit cancellation (dimensional analysis).

## 4. Difficulty Calibration
*   **Easy (1-3):** Direct application of BODMAS, simplifying fractions, identifying prime factors.
*   **Medium (4-6):**
    *   Percentage multipliers with reverse steps.
    *   Density/Volume unit conversions (cm³ vs m³).
    *   Conjugate rationalization.
*   **Hard (7-9):**
    *   Counterexample generation (Logic).
    *   Telescoping series with surds.
    *   Complex expression construction from word problems involving 4+ variables.

## 5. Key Vocabulary
*   "Simplify fully": Implies fraction arithmetic and prime factor cancellation.
*   "Proportionality": Direct vs Inverse (e.g., $P \propto R^2 T^4$).
*   "Highest Common Factor": Implies looking for shared prime factors between algebraic terms or integers.
