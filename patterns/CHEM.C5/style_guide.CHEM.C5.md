# ESAT Chemistry C5: Redox Question Style Guide

## Question Structure Patterns
1.  **"Which of the following?" Selection (60%):** Present 3-4 unbalanced or balanced chemical equations. Ask to identify which represent redox, oxidation only, or specific agent types.
    *   *Pattern:* `Which of the following [is/are] redox reactions? 1. [Eq] 2. [Eq] 3. [Eq]` followed by combination options (A: 1 only, B: 1 and 2...).
2.  **Stoichiometric Identification (30%):** Provide a narrative description of a reaction (e.g., "3 moles of Cu produces 4 moles of water") and ask for the identity of a product or the oxidation state change.
3.  **Calculation/Estimation (10%):** Combine redox concepts with gas volumes (1 mole = 24 dm³) or mass calculations (moles = mass/Mr).

## Difficulty Calibration
*   **Band 1-3 (Easy):** Simple definitions. Identify oxidation/reduction from obvious electron transfer (e.g., `Mg -> Mg2+ + 2e-`) or obvious O transfer.
*   **Band 4-6 (Medium):** Identification of agents in full reactions. Balancing simple redox equations involving common species (dichromate, permanganate, halogens).
*   **Band 7-9 (Hard):** Disproportionation in complex ions (S4O6^2-). Balancing equations where the product is unknown (deducing N-oxide from moles data). Multi-step calculations involving gas volume + redox identification.

## Wording Conventions
*   **Precise Terminology:** Uses "oxidising agent" and "reducing agent" rather than "is oxidised".
*   **State Symbols:** Frequently includes (s), (l), (g), (aq) to aid clarity or confuse (e.g., `concentrated aq` vs `dilute aq`).
*   **Explicit Electron Transfer:** Involves half-equations explicitly (e.g., `Cl2 + 2e- -> 2Cl-`) to test if students know this *isn't* a full redox reaction.
*   **Disproportionation cues:** Phrases like "two of the following sulfur atoms..." or "possible oxidation states of the four..."

## Calculator-Free Arithmetic Patterns
*   **Gas Volume:** Always uses 24 dm³ (rtp) or 1.013 x 10^5 Pa / 273 K data implicitly (often explicitly stated "1 mole = 24 dm³").
*   **Mass Calculations:** Uses simple molar masses (H=1, C=12, N=14, O=16). Often involves rounding or simple fractions (e.g., Mr of NO2 = 46).
*   **Ratios:** Answers are often simple whole number ratios (e.g., 5:1, 1:2) derived from balancing electrons.
    *   *Example Strategy:* Set up a disproportionation where `n e-` are lost per mole. Ratio often reduces to `n` or `n/2`.
