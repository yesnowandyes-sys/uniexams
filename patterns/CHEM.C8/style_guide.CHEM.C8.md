### ESAT CHEM.C8 Style Guide

**Question Structure Patterns**
*   **Data-Heavy Interpretation:** Questions frequently provide tables of physical data (Boiling Points, Densities, Rf values) and require students to apply this data to a procedural setup (Q4, Q7, Q8, Q10).
*   **Process Visualization:** Questions rely heavily on the student's ability to visualize or interpret a diagram of a setup (chromatograms, fractionating columns, separating funnels) and link it to abstract concepts (mobile phase, volatility, density).
*   **Multi-Step Logic:** Common in the "harder" questions (e.g., Q7, Q10). Students must filter information through multiple criteria:
    1.  Identify separation type (e.g., immiscible = funnel).
    2.  Apply physical property (density) to identify layer.
    3.  Apply second property (BP) to determine purification method.

**Difficulty Calibration**
*   **Band 1-3 (Recall):** Identifying components of chromatography (mobile/stationary phases), defining Rf value. *Example: Q3 (Statement 2).*
*   **Band 4-6 (Application):** Calculating Rf values, determining order of collection in distillation based on data tables, simple separation schematics. *Example: Q4, Q6, Q9.*
*   **Band 7-9 (Synthesis/Evaluation):** Combining density and boiling point data for liquid mixtures, interpreting 2D chromatograms, predicting temperature profiles in fractionating columns. *Example: Q2, Q7, Q10.*

**Wording Conventions**
*   **Precision in Phases:** rigorous use of "mobile phase" vs "stationary phase". "Solvent front" is explicitly defined.
*   **Qualitative Descriptors:** Uses "strongest attraction" (Q6) or "least mobile" (Q5) to test conceptual understanding of intermolecular forces relative to the separation mechanism.
*   **Temporal References:** "X minutes after the start... Five minutes later..." (Q9). Used to test dynamic understanding of process rates rather than static snapshots.

**Calculator-Free Arithmetic Patterns**
*   **Ratio Calculations:** Simple fractions (e.g., 12/15 = 4/5 = 0.8). *Q6, Q9.*
*   **Linear Scaling:** `New Distance = Old Distance + (Rate * Time)`. Usually simplified to integers (e.g., 15 + 10 = 25 total solvent travel, then spot travel is calculated as a ratio).
*   **Data Sorting:** Ordering lists of 2-4 items based on numerical values in a table (BP/MP). No complex stats required.

**Visual/Diagrammatic Conventions**
*   **Chromatograms:** Drawn to relative scale (mostly). Solvent front indicated by a line. Spots are distinct dots. "Not to scale" warnings are rare but present in older NSAA variants; generally, assume vertical distances are comparable but spot intensity is qualitative.
*   **Tables:** Standard scientific formatting. Units provided in headers ($^\circ$C, g cm$^{-3}$).
