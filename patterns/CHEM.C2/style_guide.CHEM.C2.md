# Style Guide — CHEM.C2 (The Periodic Table)

- Module: Chemistry (C)
- Corpus questions classified under this topic: 6
- corpus_backed: True

---

**Question Structure Patterns**
*   **Stem Format:** Predominantly "Which of the following statements is/are correct?" with 3 numbered sub-questions (1, 2, 3) and 8 fixed options (A-H). This is the standard NSAA/ESAT format for this topic.
*   **Item Independence:** The numbered statements (1, 2, 3) often test different skills:
    *   Statement 1: Chemical properties (formulas, reactivity, bonding).
    *   Statement 2: Physical properties (boiling/melting points, ionization energies).
    *   Statement 3: Quantitative reasoning (isotope abundance).
*   **Data Representation:** Questions often provide a small dataset (e.g., 5 ionisation energy values in Q6) and require the student to map them to element symbols, rather than calculating them.

**Difficulty Calibration**
*   **Core (Low Demand):** Recall of Group numbers for Alkali Metals (1), Alkaline Earth (2), Halogens (17), Noble Gases (18) and basic Period/Block identification (s, p, d).
*   **Medium (Standard Demand):** Explanation of trends (Ionisation Energy, Reactivity) using concepts of shielding, nuclear charge, and distance from the nucleus. Note: Reactivity trends must be inverted for non-metals vs metals (e.g., Reactivity decreases down Group 17).
*   **High (Advanced Demand):**
    *   **Quantitative Isotopes:** Calculating abundance percentages (e.g., Q3, Q5) where simple intuition fails.
    *   **Trend Exceptions:** Identifying specific anomalies in IE data (Mg vs Al, P vs S) rather than just stating the general rule.

**Wording Conventions**
*   **Precision:** Uses "atoms of element Z" rather than just "Z". Uses "pure sample" and "relative atomic mass" explicitly.
*   **Phrasing:** "Consider only the first three metals..." (Q1) sets artificial boundaries to test knowledge of the order Li-Na-K or F-Cl-Br without needing the whole table.

**Calculator-Free Arithmetic Patterns**
*   **Isotope Math:** Questions involving relative atomic mass ($A_r$) and isotopes are designed to be solvable without a calculator.
    *   *Example Strategy:* The weighted average is almost always the midpoint or a simple decimal (e.g., 69.7).
    *   *Calculation:* For masses 69 and 71 giving 69.7, the distance from the mean is 0.3 and 0.7. The ratio is inverse (0.7:0.3 or 7:3). The student might need to verify if "More than 70%" is true or false.
    *   *Pattern:* If options are percentages, they are usually integers (e.g., 25%, 50%, 75%) or simple thresholds to compare against.
