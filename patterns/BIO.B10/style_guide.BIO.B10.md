# ESAT Biology B10: Ecosystems Style Guide

## 1. Question Architecture & Complexity
*   **Multi-Stage Reasoning:** Questions in this topic rarely ask for a single fact recall. They typically follow a `Data Presentation -> Biological Mechanism -> Quantitative/Logical Deduction` path.
    *   *Example (Q8/Q4):* Read graph shape $\rightarrow$ Link to tolerance of species (Bloodworm vs Stonefly) $\rightarrow$ Deduce location based on oxygen levels.
*   **Process-Oriented Diagrams:** "Label the process" questions (Q2) are rare compared to "Identify the *role* of the process" questions (e.g., involves enzymes vs combustion).
*   **Scenario Application:** Abstract definitions (e.g., intraspecific competition) are almost always tested via concrete novel examples (e.g., beetles farming fungi, deer fighting) rather than definition matching.

## 2. Data Visualization
*   **Predator-Prey Graphs:** Phase-shift logic (Predator peaks follow Prey peaks) is standard, but questions often test *deviations* from the ideal cycle (e.g., Q1 checks if a specific year contradicts the 'Prey > Predator' rule).
*   **Kite Diagrams / Transects:** Expect kite diagrams (Q13, Q14) showing abundance/distribution. Questions require extracting "Number of individuals" from "Width of bar" or "Centred distance".
*   **Energy Pyramids:** Often represented as flow charts or circular diagrams (Q16, Q19) rather than pyramids. Values are given in Kilojoules (kJ) or arbitrary units (a.u.).
*   **Pollution Curves:** The "Oxygen Sag Curve" (Dissolved Oxygen vs Distance downstream) is a recurring motif (Q4, Q8).

## 3. Quantitative & Calculator-Free Patterns
*   **Arithmetic:**
    *   Simple percentages of large numbers ($10\%$ of $150\,000$).
    *   Ratios (e.g., 1 unit energy to X units biomass).
    *   Summation of counts (Quadrats).
*   **Rounding:**
    *   Expect clean numbers: $10\%$ efficiency, $50\%$ loss.
    *   In Q5, the calculation involves dividing by 10 (10 quadrats).
    *   In Q19, calculating $60\%$ of 100 units is trivial; calculating $22.5\%$ of $40$ units involves slightly harder arithmetic ($40 \times 0.225 = 9$).
*   **Units:**
    *   Common units: $\text{m}^2$ (Quadrats), $\text{kJ/m}^2$ (Energy), $\text{m}^3$ (Water volume).
    *   Conversions are generally provided or limited to $cm \to m$ ($100 \text{ cm} = 1 \text{ m}$).

## 4. Wording & Distractor Phrasing
*   **"Always" and "Never":** Statements using absolutes (e.g., Q1 "always lower") are frequent traps.
*   **"Can be concluded":** Requires strict logic. If the graph *shows* a trend but implies a cause (e.g., Q4 attributing oxygen drop to algae), it is often invalid if the mechanism doesn't fit.
*   **"Which of the following statements...":** The standard format for Q1-Q22. Almost always a "Select all that apply" format disguised as multiple choice (A-H combinations).

## 5. Difficulty Calibration
*   **Band 1-3:** Basic definitions of symbiosis, identifying trophic levels from a food chain.
*   **Band 4-6:** Interpreting Predator-Prey phase shifts, explaining the Carbon Cycle processes (Respiration vs Combustion).
*   **Band 7-9:** Designing/evaluating sampling methodologies (Q9, Q10), calculating energy flow efficiency with assimilation vs consumption nuances (Q16), interpreting complex pollution graphs with multiple species dependencies (Q8).
