# Style Guide for CHEM.C15 (Kinetic/Particle Theory)

## Contextual Framing
*   **Experimental Scenarios:** Questions are rarely abstract. They typically describe a specific laboratory setup (e.g., flasks in a box, diffusion experiments) to ground the theory.
*   **Constants & Variables:** Questions explicitly define conditions (e.g., "atmospheric pressure," "uniform temperature") to isolate the specific variable being tested (mass, speed, time).
*   **Data Representation:** Students should expect prompts describing the detection of substances over time, requiring them to translate physical movement into temporal order.

## Conceptual Depth
*   **Micro-Macro Links:** The core skill tested is connecting microscopic properties (mass of a particle, speed of a single molecule) to macroscopic observations (time of detection, temperature of the system).
*   **Formula Logic:** While calculation is required, it is often mental estimation or logic-based.
    *   *Example:* Instead of asking "Calculate the speed in m/s," the question asks "Which gas arrives first?" or "Is X 2 times or 4 times faster than Y?"
    *   This requires rearranging $v = \sqrt{2E/m}$ to compare relative values rather than absolute ones.

## Assessment Logic
*   **Statement Evaluation:** The standard format (seen in 100% of current corpus) is the "Statement evaluation" (Best Answer / Which is correct?). This tests discrimination between plausible sounding physics and actual physical laws.
*   **Distractor Density:** Distractors are highly specific, usually intermixing correct definitions (e.g., "Temperature is proportional to KE") with incorrect conclusions (e.g., "...therefore every particle has the same KE").

## Calculator-Free Arithmetic
*   **Approximation:** Students must be comfortable approximating square roots of integers (e.g., knowing $\sqrt{5} \approx 2.23$ or $\sqrt{2} \approx 1.41$) to eliminate incorrect quantitative options.
*   **Ratio Math:** Problems rely on ratios (e.g., Mass A vs Mass B) rather than SI units (kg, J).
