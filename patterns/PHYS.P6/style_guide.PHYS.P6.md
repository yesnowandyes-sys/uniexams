# ESAT PHYS.P6 (Waves) Style Guide

## 1. Structural Patterns

*   **Hybrid Contexts:** Questions rarely test "waves" in isolation. The standard ESAT style combines wave mechanics with:
    *   **Kinematics:** Moving ships encountering waves (Q7, Q33), radar traps (Q21).
    *   **Optics/Reflection:** Light in glass blocks (Q42, Q45), mirror rotations (Q39).
    *   **Material Science:** Ultrasound in metals/gels (Q17, Q37).
*   **Graph-Text-Image Mapping:** Highly prevalent.
    *   **Displacement-Time:** Used to extract $T$ or $f$.
    *   **Displacement-Distance:** Used to extract $\lambda$.
    *   **Longitudinal Diagrams:** 'Dot' diagrams showing particle density are common (Q43, Q50). Students must translate density gradients to sinusoidal displacements.

## 2. Difficulty Calibration

*   **Band 1-3 (Recall/Knowledge):**
    *   Definitions of amplitude, wavelength, period.
    *   Standard Wave Equation $v = f \lambda$.
    *   EM Spectrum ordering (e.g., identifying Microwave vs Gamma).
    *   *Example:* Q3 (calculating UV frequency range).
*   **Band 4-6 (Application/Calculation):**
    *   **Particle vs Wave Motion:** The "total distance travelled by a particle" question is a staple. It requires the specific formula $Distance = 4A \times f \times t$.
    *   **Unit Conversion:** Heavy use of mixed units (cm, m, ms, s, nm, Hz). Errors here are a primary differentiator.
    *   **Radar/Sonar:** Depth calculation $d = vt/2$.
*   **Band 7-9 (Synthesis/Novelty):**
    *   **Relative Velocity Waves:** Moving source/observer (or ship) requiring $v_{relative} = v_{wave} \pm v_{source}$.
    *   **Stationary Waves:** Pipes/Strings (Q11, Q13). Requires visualising harmonics and relating $L$ to $\lambda/2$ or $\lambda/4$.
    *   **Interference/Phase:** Path difference calculations (Q28, Q46). Requires integer logic ($n\lambda$ vs $(n+0.5)\lambda$).
    *   **Refraction Geometry:** Visualising wavefronts changing speed and direction (Q29, Q47).

## 3. Wording & Arithmetic Conventions

*   **Significant Figures:** Inputs are often given to 2 or 3 significant figures (e.g., $3.0 \times 10^8$, $5.0$ Hz). Answers usually reflect this precision.
*   **Unit Handling:**
    *   Time is often in **milliseconds (ms)**. Distance often in **cm** or **km**.
    *   Speed of sound is consistently **320, 330, 340, or 500 m/s** depending on the context (Air vs Water/Metal).
    *   Speed of light is **$3.0 \times 10^8$ m/s**.
*   **Question Phrasing:**
    *   "What is the total distance travelled by a particle..." (Shorthand for the 4A-f-t calculation).
    *   "Which row in the table..." (Used frequently for multi-variable comparisons, e.g., Q27, Q30).
    *   "What can be concluded..." (Tests logical deduction from ranges).

## 4. Calculator-Free Arithmetic Patterns

*   **Multiplication by 4:** The $4A$ factor in particle distance questions (Q1, Q4).
*   **Powers of 10:** EM spectrum questions rely on exponent arithmetic ($10^{-7}$ to $10^{-9}$, etc.).
*   **Fractional Speeds:** Ratios like $2/3$, $1/2$, $3/4$ are common for refractive indices or relative speeds (Q6, Q41, Q44).
*   **Phase Angles:** Calculating path difference $\Delta x$ and converting to phase $\phi = \frac{2\pi}{\lambda}\Delta x$ often involves simple fractions like $\pi/2$ or $\pi/4$ (Q49).
