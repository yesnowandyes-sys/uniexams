# ESAT MATHS2.MM8 (Graphs of Functions) Style Guide

## 1. Question Structure & Typology
Questions in this domain are predominantly **visual-analytical** or **numerical-graphical**.

*   **Visual/Graphical Selection (~50%)**: Students are given a plotted graph (A-F) and must select the one corresponding to an equation (Q7, Q9, Q11) or a transformation description (Q2).
*   **Root Counting / Intersection Analysis (~30%)**: Students must determine the number of solutions to equations like $f(x) = g(x)$, often without solving explicitly, by analyzing graphs or properties (Q3, Q4, Q5, Q6, Q10).
*   **Logic/Domain-Range (~10%)**: Abstract questions testing the understanding of function definitions and logic (Q8).
*   **Max/Min Optimization (~10%)**: Finding extremal values of composite functions (Q1).

## 2. Calibration of Difficulty
*   **Band 1-3 (Easy)**: Recognition of standard shapes ($x^2, e^x, \sin x$) and simple single-transformations (e.g., vertical shifts).
*   **Band 4-6 (Medium)**:
    *   **Hybrid Functions**: Combining polynomial and exponential features (Q4, Q7).
    *   **Composition**: Functions involving $f(x) \pm c$ inside exponents or trig functions (Q1).
    *   **Intersection Logic**: Determining the number of roots for $f(x)=g(x)$ where standard methods fail (Q3).
*   **Band 7-9 (Hard)**:
    *   **Reverse Engineering**: Identifying transformations from a final plot (Q2).
    *   **Parametric Constraints**: Finding ranges of $k$ for specific intersection behaviors (Q5, Q10).
    *   **Complex Sketches**: Graphs defined implicitly or with unusual compositions like $\sin(x^2+y^2)$ (Q9) or $u(x)^{v(x)}$ (Q11).

## 3. Wording Conventions
*   **"Which one of the following..."**: Standard for graph selection.
*   **"How many solutions..."**: Standard for counting intersections.
*   **"Complete range of values"**: Used in hardest questions (Q5).
*   **"Dotted lines indicate asymptotes"**: Explicit visual aid convention (Q7).

## 4. Calculator-Free Arithmetic Patterns
*   **Key Values**: Calculations rely on integer outputs (0, 1, -1).
*   **Special Angles**: $0, 30, 45, 60, 90, 180, 270, 360$ degrees.
*   **Integers**: Intersection points or asymptotes are almost always at integer coordinates (e.g., asymptote at $y=1$, roots at $x=0,1,2$).
*   **Properties over Calculation**: Questions favor determining the *sign* of a value or the *existence* of a root rather than calculating a precise irrational value.
