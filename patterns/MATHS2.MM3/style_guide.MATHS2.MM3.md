# ESAT Mathematics 2 — Coordinate Geometry Style Guide

## 1. Structural Patterns
**Multi-Step Algebraic-Geometric Hybrid**
Questions in this topic rarely ask for a simple formula application (e.g., "find the equation of the line"). Instead, they combine coordinate algebra with geometric constraints or calculus concepts.
*   *Example (Q3):* Straight line equation $\to$ perpendicular line equation $\to$ area of triangle.
*   *Example (Q5):* Quadratic curve $\to$ intersection with x-axis $\to$ rectangle construction.

**Embedded Functionality**
Coordinate geometry is often used as a *tool* to solve problems framed as other topics.
*   *Example (Q4):* Framed as a curve intersection, but requires knowledge of the relationship between quadratic roots ($q-p$) and coefficients.
*   *Example (Q23):* Framed as "shortest distance," requiring optimization (differentiation) of a distance function derived from coordinate geometry.

## 2. Difficulty Calibration
*   **Band 1-3 (Foundation):** Finding parallel/perpendicular gradients, calculating midpoints, simple circle equations from given center/radius.
*   **Band 4-6 (Core):** Using perpendicular distance formulas, deriving circle equations from geometric constraints (squares/tangents), linking quadratic discriminants to intersection geometry.
*   **Band 7-9 (Advanced):**
    *   **Reflections/Transformations:** Analyzing reflected gradients (Q2) or rotated curves (Q28).
    *   **Variable Geometry:** Points defined by variables with constraints (e.g., $AC = 2AB$ in Q1).
    *   **Abstract Proof/Conditions:** Identifying necessary and sufficient conditions for circle tangency (Q31) or concurrency (Q39).

## 3. Wording & Convention Conventions
*   **Precision:** "The straight line with equation..." is preferred over "The line...".
*   **Signposting:** Constraints are usually explicit: "where $m > 0$", "non-zero gradient".
*   **Units:** Area units are explicitly mentioned ("square units").
*   **Notation:** Standard vector notation $\begin{pmatrix} x \\ y \end{pmatrix}$ appears in coordinate geometry contexts involving movement (Q19).

## 4. Calculator-Free Arithmetic Patterns
*   **Integer Coefficients:** Most line equations utilize integers (e.g., $2x + 3y + 1 = 0$ in Q12).
*   **Surds:** Answers involving distances or circle radii often leave the answer in surd form (e.g., $\sqrt{5}$, $\frac{2\sqrt{5}}{5}a$ in Q6).
*   **Fractions:** Gradients and intercepts often involve simple fractions (e.g., $-\frac{13}{6}$ in Q8).
*   **Avoidance of Decimals:** With the exception of approximate values in "closest to" questions (Q11), exact forms are preferred.

## 5. Distractor Strategy
Distractors often serve as "checkpoints" for common algebraic errors:
*   Sign errors ($x+h$ vs $x-h$).
*   Perpendicular gradient errors ($m$ vs $-1/m$).
*   Formula misapplications (using arc length formulas for area, or vice versa).
