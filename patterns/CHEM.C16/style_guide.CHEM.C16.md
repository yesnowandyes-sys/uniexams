# Style Guide — CHEM.C16 (Chemical tests)

- Module: Chemistry (C)
- Corpus questions classified under this topic: 2
- corpus_backed: False

## Question Structure Patterns
Questions in this topic are **Multi-step Deductive** problems.
*   **Q1 Structure:** Identification of Cation (via precipitate colour) + Identification of Anion (via precipitate formation with BaCl₂) + Stoichiometry (molar mass calculation).
*   **Q2 Structure:** Evaluation of Discriminatory Power. Candidates are given a set of species and must evaluate three distinct qualitative tests to see which one yields unique outcomes for *every* species.

## Difficulty Calibration
*   **Moderate to High:** Questions require bridging qualitative observation (colour change, precipitate) with quantitative calculation (stoichiometry) or logical set-theory (distinguishing 3 items).
*   **Q1 (Hard):** Requires knowledge of specific precipitate colours (Brown = Fe³⁺) and white sulfate precipitates, combined with formula mass calculation involving non-1 stoichiometry (Fe₂(SO₄)₃).
*   **Q2 (Medium):** Tests knowledge of cation/anion tests (Flame tests, Hydroxide ppt, Sulfate ppt) and logic (Process of Elimination).

## Wording Conventions
*   **Standardization:** Phrases like "an aqueous solution of...", "in the presence of dilute nitric/hydrochloric acid", and "turns cloudy" are standard ESAT/NSAA terminology.
*   **Implicit Stoichiometry:** Q1 implies formula derivation without asking "What is the formula?". It jumps straight to "What is the relative molar mass?", forcing the student to derive the intermediate formula.

## Calculator-Free Arithmetic Patterns
*   **Rounding:** Atomic masses often provided or approximated (e.g., Cl=35.5).
*   **Multiplication:**
    *   Fe₂(SO₄)₃ calculation requires: 2(56) + 3(32 + 4(16)).
    *   112 + 3(96) = 112 + 288 = 400.
    *   Q2 options involved integer logic (evaluating truth of 3 statements), minimizing calculation but maximizing logical load.
*   **Difference Engineering:** Incorrect options often represent the molar mass of the *other* common ion species (e.g., FeSO₄ = 152 vs Fe₂(SO₄)₃ = 400) to catch students who get the chemistry right but the formula wrong.
