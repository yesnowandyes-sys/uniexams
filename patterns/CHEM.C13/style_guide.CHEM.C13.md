# ESAT Chemistry (C13) Style Guide

## 1. Structural Formulae Convention
*   **Display Preference:** Questions predominantly use **displayed structural formulae** (showing all bonds explicitly) or **skeletal formulae** over condensed text, except for very simple molecules (e.g., `CH3CH=CH2`).
*   **Stereochemistry:** 3D arrangement is often implied in 2D via grouping (e.g., `(CH3)2C=`).
*   **Complexity:** Structures frequently involve branching (methyl/ethyl groups) attached to the main functional chain, testing recognition of the "longest chain" vs "substituents" (e.g., `C(CH3)2` vs `C(C2H5)`).

## 2. Arithmetic & Quantitative Patterns
*   **Atom Economy:** Questions often require balancing internal knowledge (functional groups, general formulas `CnH2n`) with provided data (Atomic masses `Ar`, Gas volumes `24 dm³`).
*   **Integer Constraints:** Molar masses and combustion volumes are almost always chosen to result in **integers** or simple fractions (e.g., 144 dm³ = 6 mol CO₂; Mr 116).
*   **Multi-step Calculus:** Commonly involves a chain: Volume -> Moles -> Mass -> Mr. Distraction options often target the breakage of one link in this chain.
*   **Approximation:** Rarely used. If `~26%` Nitrogen is given, it usually implies a fraction like `2/7` or `14/53` that fits exactly with integer atomic masses.

## 3. Wording & Logic
*   **Negative Constraints:** Frequent use of negative descriptors to force conceptual elimination: "does *not* decolourise bromine," "does *not* produce a silver mirror," "has *no* stereoisomers." This requires the student to map properties to functional groups rigorously.
*   **Functional Group Logic:** Questions often rely on reactivity chains: "React with X -> Product Y, which then reacts with Z." The student must track the functional group state through each step.
*   **Percentage Constraints:** Identification via composition (e.g., `~14.3% H` -> implies 1/7 mass ratio -> Cycloalkane logic).

## 4. Difficulty Calibration
*   **Band 1-3:** Recall of homologous series formulas (`CnH2n+2`), naming C1-C6, simple fractional distillation trends.
*   **Band 4-6:** Identification of isomers (structural and stereoisomers), reaction mechanisms (electrophilic addition), stoichiometry with gases.
*   **Band 7-9:** Multi-step synthesis logic (deducing structure P from product U), reactivity analysis of all isomers of a given formula (e.g., "How many of the C5 isomers..."), and applying the "Degree of Unsaturation" rule to combustion data to identify rings/pi-bonds.
