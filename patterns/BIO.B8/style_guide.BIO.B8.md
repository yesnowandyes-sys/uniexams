# Style Guide — BIO.B8 (Enzymes)

- Module: Biology (B)
- Corpus questions classified under this topic: 22
- corpus_backed: True

---

## Question Structure Patterns

**1. The "Triple-Statement" Evaluation**
*   **Frequency:** ~80% of questions.
*   **Structure:** A scenario (graph, experiment setup, or table) is presented. Three numbered statements (1, 2, 3) follow, which are potential interpretations or causes.
*   **Task:** Select the option (A-H) indicating which combination of statements is correct (e.g., "2 and 3 only").
*   **Example:** *Q1, Q5, Q6, Q9, Q10...*
*   **Rationale:** Allows efficient testing of multiple distinct concepts (e.g., pH effect, substrate saturation, and enzyme specificity) within a single multiple-choice framework.

**2. Visual Data Interpretation**
*   **Frequency:** ~50% of questions.
*   **Structure:** A line graph or bar chart is the central stimulus. Usually plotting Mass/Concentration/Rate vs. Time/pH/Temp.
*   **Key Features:**
    *   **Asymptotic Plateaus:** Used frequently to imply limiting factors (substrate depletion) or completion of reaction.
    *   **Bell Curves:** Used for Temperature/pH vs. Rate.
    *   **Disjointed Lines:** Comparing Condition X vs. Condition Y on one axis.
*   **Calculator-Free Arithmetic:**
    *   **Gradient Reading:** Students often calculate "Initial Rate" by drawing a tangent or estimating $\Delta y / \Delta x$ for the first linear section (Q13, Q15).
    *   **Percentage Change:** Simple integer math (e.g., Q2: $80 \to 20$ is a 75% decrease). Values are chosen to be divisible or easily estimable (often multiples of 10 or 25).
    *   **Table Trends:** Questions using tables (Q2, Q17) typically involve identifying the "limiting" region where values plateau.

**3. Experimental Design Variables**
*   **Structure:** A list of physical/chemical factors is provided (pH, concentration, temperature, stirring).
*   **Task:** Identify which factors *could* explain an observed difference in rate/time.
*   **Style:** Often asks "Which of the following could have affected...?" (Q5).
*   **Triggers:** Look for "All other variables were kept constant" (Q1, Q17).

## Wording Conventions

*   **Active Voice & Agency:** Enzymes are often personified in distractors ("enzyme dies"), but correct answers use passive or structural language ("bonds break", "active site shape changes").
*   **Qualitative Descriptors:** Frequently uses "higher", "lower", "optimum", "limiting factor". Quantitative descriptors are usually integers (e.g., "pH 7", "37°C", "0.5 mol dm⁻³").
*   **Inhibitor Logic:** Questions rarely explicitly name "competitive/non-competitive inhibition" terminology but describe the *scenario* of it (e.g., boiling an enzyme to denature it).
*   **Specificity:** Names specific enzymes (Catalase, Amylase, Pepsin, Trypsin) and substrates (Starch, Hydrogen Peroxide, Protein). Students must know the pairings.
*   **Direction of Change:** Wording focuses on **increases/decreases** in rate or **time taken**. A common trap is swapping "Time taken" (inverse) with "Rate" (direct).

## Difficulty Calibration

*   **Easy (Band 1-3):**
    *   Recall of specific enzymes/substrates (e.g., Amylase + Starch).
    *   Identifying the "optimum" peak on a bell curve.
    *   Calculating simple percentage decreases from a table.
    *   Knowing boiling denatures enzymes.
*   **Medium (Band 4-6):**
    *   Explaining *why* the rate drops after the optimum (distinguishing kinetic energy from denaturation).
    *   Understanding that enzymes are not used up (catalyst definition).
    *   Interpreting plateau phases in graphs (substrate limitation vs. enzyme saturation).
    *   Analyzing "Time Taken" graphs (inverse relationships).
*   **Hard (Band 7-9):**
    *   Inferring mechanism from a "Product vs. Time" graph with two lines (X and Y) where one plateaus earlier (Q1, Q11).
    *   Connecting pH changes to color indicators in a broader system (Q19).
    *   Evaluating "2 and 3 only" style questions where two abstract concepts must be assessed simultaneously (e.g., Mutations + pH gradients in Q6).
    *   Understanding the effect of volume vs. concentration (Q3).
