## Assessment Patterns for Exponentials and Logarithms

### 1. Question Typology & Frequency
The corpus reveals a distinct hierarchy of question types for this topic:
*   **Type A: Pure Index Laws (Approx. 20%):** Complex manipulation of bases (converting $9 \to 3^2$) and simplification of fractional expressions with variable exponents (e.g., Q1, Q8).
*   **Type B: Log Laws & Algebraic Substitution (Approx. 35%):** "Expansion" or "Collapse" questions where candidates must move between $k \ln x$ and $\ln x^k$, or solve for $x$ in terms of logs (e.g., Q10, Q36).
*   **Type C: Quadratic Mapping (Approx. 30%):** Equations of the form $a^{2x} + \dots = 0$ or $(\log x)^2 + \dots = 0$ (e.g., Q6, Q9).
*   **Type D: Transcendental Functions & Inequalities (Approx. 15%):** Comparing values like $2^{\sqrt{x}}$ vs $(\sqrt{2})^x$ or analyzing $a^{\cos x}$ (e.g., Q21, Q30, Q32).

### 2. Difficulty Calibration
*   **Easy (Band 1-3):** Base conversion of simple integers (2, 3, 4, 8, 9) and direct application of $\log_a + \log_b = \log(ab)$. Numbers are chosen to be mentally calculable (e.g., Q1: $9=3^2$).
*   **Medium (Band 4-6):** Introduction of negative exponents, fractional bases, or "hidden" quadratics requiring substitution ($u = 3^x$). Domain restrictions (x > 0) begin to eliminate answers here.
*   **Hard (Band 7-9):** "Nested" functions ($\log(\log x)$), inequalities with changing bases (reversing inequality signs for bases $<1$), or simultaneous log equations (Q25, Q31).

### 3. Wording & Structural Conventions
*   **"Which one of the following...":** Standard for Multiple Choice. The phrasing "equivalent to" implies simplification, while "solution to" implies solving for a variable.
*   **"Real numbers" vs "Positive real numbers":** Context is crucial. Questions often specify $x>0$ implicitly by using $\log x$, but valid checks for domain occur in the distractors.
*   **"Simplification":** Often implies a single term expression (e.g., $3^{4n}$) versus an expanded series.

### 4. Calculator-Free Arithmetic Patterns
*   **Base Selection:** Bases are overwhelmingly powers of 2 or 3 ($2, 3, 4, 8, 9, 16, 27, 32, 81$). This allows students to mentally convert to a common base (usually 2 or 3).
*   **Log-Integer Arithmetic:** Arguments of logs are often related by multiplication (e.g., 5, 10, 20, 40) or powers ($\log_2 8$), allowing the use of integer arithmetic rather than calculator estimation.
*   **Constants:** In questions like $2^5 \approx 3^3$ (Q39), the approximation is explicitly provided to facilitate mental comparison.

### 5. Common "Trap" Structures
*   **The "Blind Spot" Trap:** Asking for the sum/product of *real* roots, where the quadratic yields a negative root that is invalid for logs (Q6, Q9).
*   **The "Coefficient" Trap:** Distractors often place the coefficient $k$ inside the log argument ($\log(kx)$) to test the power law rigorously.
