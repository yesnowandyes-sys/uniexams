# ESAT Chemistry Topic C7: Group Chemistry Style Guide

## Quantitative Complexity & Calculator-Free Arithmetic
*   **Atomic Mass Simplification:** Questions rely on simplified standard atomic weights ($A_r$) provided in the stem (e.g., Li=7, Br=80, Ca=40).
*   **Arithmetic Level:**
    *   **Simple Ratios:** Stoichiometry involves 1:1 or 1:2 ratios (e.g., Q4 Titration: $50 \text{ cm}^3$ of $0.2 \text{ mol/dm}^3$ vs $400 \text{ cm}^3$ of $0.05 \text{ mol/dm}^3$). The math is designed to be mental-friendly ($0.2 \times 50 = 10$ mmol).
    *   **Percentage Calculations:** Simple "percent by mass" calculations using round numbers (e.g., Q2: Ca=40, Br=80 -> $80/(40+80+80) \approx$ easy fraction).
    *   **Molar Mass Estimation:** Determining moles involves dividing by roughly 40 or 80 (simple division).

## Question Structure Patterns
1.  **The "Trend & Verify" Stem:**
    *   Format: "Which of the following statements [1, 2, 3] is/are correct?"
    *   Content: A mix of physical property trends (MP/BP), chemical reactivity trends (displacement), and math-verification (percentage mass).
    *   Complexity: Requires checking 3 independent facts. Common in S1 (Q1, Q2).
2.  **The "Multi-Step Deduction" (S2 Style):**
    *   Format: Paragraph of experimental context -> Logical deduction of identity -> Calculation of result.
    *   Steps:
        1.  Identify an element 'X' using qualitative knowledge (e.g., "most soluble hydroxide" -> identifies X as Barium).
        2.  Perform a quantitative calculation (Titration/Neutralization).
        3.  Select the option that matches *both* the identity and the math.
    *   Example: Q4 (Identify X -> Titrate Y -> Choose correct product moles).

## Wording Conventions & Triggers
*   **Reactivity vs. Oxidizing:**
    *   Phrasing: "Oxidise chloride ions to form chlorine." This is a coded way of asking "Is this element more reactive than chlorine?" Students must translate "Oxidise X to X" into "Displace X".
*   **Mass Change Directions:**
    *   Phrasing: "Final mass... compared to initial mass."
    *   Triggers: "Open beakers" (implies gas can escape).
    *   Implication: Requires analyzing the net mass flow (Mass of metal added + Mass of gas absorbed - Mass of gas released).
*   **Aqueous Contexts:**
    *   Explicitly states "aqueous solution" to imply the presence of water and potential for ion displacement or neutralization.

## Difficulty Calibration
*   **Band 4-6 (Standard):**
    *   Recall of trends (MP/BP up/down groups).
    *   Reactivity series recall (Li < Na < K; F < Cl < Br).
    *   Simple 1-step stoichiometry ($c \times v$).
*   **Band 7-9 (Advanced):**
    *   Q3-style comparative analysis: Holding a variable (mass of metal) constant across a group and comparing the *magnitude* of mass change in a reaction. This combines stoichiometry ($n(\text{H}_2)$ produced vs $n(\text{M})$ used) with atomic mass trends.
    *   Q4-style deduction: Combining solubility rules (Group 2 Hydroxides) with titration math.
