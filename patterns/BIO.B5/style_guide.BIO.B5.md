# Style Guide — BIO.B5 (DNA)

- Module: Biology (B)
- Corpus questions classified under this topic: 10
- corpus_backed: True

---

## Arithmetic and Calculation Patterns
*   **Integer Base Math:** Questions involving base counts (e.g., 4500 pairs, 420 pairs) are designed to yield clean integers. Common percentages are 10%, 20%, 25%, and 50% to allow for mental math (e.g., 10% of 4500 = 450).
*   **Percentage Symmetry:** Almost all percentage questions rely on Chargaff's rules ($A=T, G=C$). If $A=10\%$, then $T=10\%$, leaving $80\%$ for $G+C$. The "trick" is usually realizing that $G$ and $C$ do not have to be $40\%$ each (only if the specific sequence demands it or if it's a theoretical organism), though questions often imply equal distribution or give data for one strand to solve the other.
*   **Hydrogen Bond Totals:** When calculating H-bonds (e.g., EcoRI cutting), the arithmetic is simple summation ($3 \times N_{GC} + 2 \times N_{AT}$). The complexity lies in counting the bases cut correctly (typically 4 bases per sticky end = 2 cuts = 8 bases total per sticky end interaction).

## Question Structure
*   **Multi-Logical Steps:** Higher difficulty questions (Q1, Q10) combine two distinct concepts.
    *   *Example:* Calculating amino acids from base pairs (division by 3) AND calculating base percentages from Chargaff's rules.
    *   *Example:* Identifying an enzyme (knowledge) AND calculating hydrogen bond totals (arithmetic).
*   **Visual Data Interpretation:** 50% of questions (Q4, Q8, Q10, Q6 loosely) use diagrams, charts, or spatial reasoning. Students must be able to translate a visual representation (pie chart, DNA diagram) into a numerical or verbal conclusion.
*   **Complementary Strand Logic:** A common format (Q2, Q8) provides data for one strand and asks for the composition of the complementary strand, requiring the mental "flip" ($A \leftrightarrow T, G \leftrightarrow C$).

## Wording Conventions
*   **Specificity:** "Coding section of DNA" vs "Non-coding". The exam explicitly signals "coding" when translation is required.
*   **Double vs Single Stranded:** Questions carefully distinguish between "a section of DNA" (implies double stranded by default in this context unless specified ssDNA) and "one strand of a section". Misinterpreting this leads to immediate failure (e.g., assuming A=T on a single strand).
*   **Bold Text:** Key specific terms like "not repeated anywhere else", "not found in other organisms", or specific base counts are often highlighted to prevent ambiguity.
