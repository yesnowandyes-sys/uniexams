# ESAT/NSAA Chemistry Electrolysis Style Guide

## Question Structure Patterns
*   **Predictions (30%):** Questions typically present a specific setup (e.g., 'concentrated aqueous NaCl', 'molten PbBr2', 'aqueous CuSO4 with carbon electrodes') and ask for the products at the anode and cathode. These often appear as table rows or combination options (e.g., Q1, Q4, Q9, Q12).
*   **Quantitative Stoichiometry (50%):** Questions provide data (gas volume, mass deposited, time, current flow rate) and require calculation of an unknown variable. These rely heavily on the assumption that 1 mole of electrons occupies a specific charge or volume of gas (e.g., Q2, Q10, Q11, Q13).
*   **Pattern Recognition/Deduction (20%):** Questions ask which statements are correct regarding general rules (oxidation states, electrode definitions) or require deducing an observation from a given half-equation (e.g., Q3, Q5, Q7).

## Difficulty Calibration
*   **Band 1-3:** Direct application of molten electrolysis rules (ions discharged are from the salt itself) or identifying definitions (anode/cathode).
*   **Band 4-6:**
    *   Aqueous electrolysis involving discharge rules (Water vs. Ion, Sulfate vs. Nitrate).
    *   Simple molar calculations (Volume of Gas A given Volume of Gas B with 1:1 or 2:1 ratios).
    *   Converting grams to moles using standard $A_r$ values.
*   **Band 7-9:**
    *   Complex quantitative scenarios involving varying oxidation states (e.g., Q11 comparing Cu vs K vs Cl).
    *   Non-integer stoichiometry or time-based electron flow rates (e.g., Q10 requiring multi-step calculation).
    *   Conceptual questions requiring integration of pH changes with half-equations (e.g., Q7).

## Wording Conventions
*   **Electrode Identification:** Questions consistently define electrodes as 'positive' and 'negative', though they often parenthesize the terms (anode/cathode). Students must be fluent in both terminologies.
*   **Phase Labels:** States of matter $(l)$, $(g)$, $(aq)$, $(s)$ are almost always included in half-equations.
*   **Standard Conditions:** Gas volume questions explicitly state 'room temperature and pressure' (RTP) and define the molar volume (usually 24 $dm^3$).

## Calculator-Free Arithmetic Patterns
*   **Ratios:** The most common pattern is the 2:1:1 ratio (2 $H_2O \rightarrow O_2$ vs $2H^+ \rightarrow H_2$). Students should expect to divide or multiply gas volumes by 2.
*   **Molar Masses:** Papers provide integer or 1-decimal $A_r$ values (e.g., Cl = 35.5, Cu = 64, Al = 27).
*   **Unit Conversion:** Questions frequently swap between grams, moles, and $dm^3$. Students must know:
    *   $Mass (g) = Moles \times A_r$
    *   $Volume (dm^3) = Moles \times 24$
*   **Constants:** Expect the Faraday constant or electron flow rate to be given in 'moles of electrons per second' (e.g., Q10) rather than Coulombs, simplifying the math to $n(e^-) = rate \times time$.
