# Topic BIO.B3 Style Guide

## Question Structure Patterns
*   **Multiple Response (Grid-Select):** The vast majority of questions (e.g., Q1, Q2, Q3, Q15, Q19) use the "Which of the following statements is/are correct?" format with 3 numbered statements and options A-H (none, 1, 2, 3, 1&2, 1&3, 2&3, All). This is the dominant mode for testing combined conceptual knowledge.
*   **Data/Graph Interpretation:** Questions frequently include a karyogram (Q2), a graph of DNA mass over time (Q10), or a life cycle diagram (Q8, Q16). Students must extract qualitative or quantitative data from the visual to answer statements.
*   **Table/Row Selection:** Questions often present a table of data (e.g., Q23 chromosome counts, Q24 DNA rates, Q4 Fruit fly sexes) and ask to identify the correct row. This tests the ability to synthesize multiple variables (chromosome number, sex, process) simultaneously.

## Difficulty Calibration
*   **Level 1 (Easy):** Direct recall of roles (Mitosis = growth/repair, Meiosis = gametes) or standard chromosome counts (Human = 46, Gamete = 23). See Q1.
*   **Level 3 (Medium):** Application of concepts to novel scenarios (e.g., Fruit fly sex determination ratios in Q4, or Ant haplodiploidy in Q16). Requires understanding the *mechanism* (X:A ratio or Haplodiploidy), not just the human fact.
*   **Level 5 (Hard):** Quantitative logic involving arithmetic or temporal reasoning.
    *   *Time-based:* Calculating nutrient requirements based on division rates (Q26).
    *   *Mean values:* Calculating average chromosome numbers in mixed samples (Q23).
    *   *Chemotherapy mapping:* Matching drug mechanisms to specific cycle stages (Q29).

## Wording Conventions
*   **Precision in "Somatic" vs "Germline":** The corpus is precise about cell types (e.g., "cheek cell", "white blood cell", "spermatogonium"). Questions often hinge on whether the cell in question can pass on mutations.
*   **"Using the information/graph...":** Explicit instruction to use provided data prevents students from using outside knowledge or assumptions.
*   **Conditionals:** Frequent use of "If..., then..." scenarios to test understanding of genetic dependencies (e.g., "If gamete Q contains a Y chromosome...").

## Calculator-Free Arithmetic Patterns
*   **Powers of 2:** Common in cell division problems ($2^n$).
    *   *Example:* 150 cells $\rightarrow$ 300 mins. 300/50 = 6 mins? No, 300/50 = 6 *divisions* (rounds). Total cells = $150 \times 2^6$. Students must recognize $2^6 = 64$.
*   **Means and Weighted Averages:**
    *   *Example:* $(23 \times 3 + 46 \times 2) / 5$. Requires simple addition/multiplication but logical setup.
*   **Ratios:** X:A ratio calculations (e.g., 2:2 is 1:1 Female).
*   **Unit Conversion:** Occasionally required (e.g., $\text{mm}^3$ to $\text{µm}^3$ or similar magnitude shifts), usually involving factors of 1000.
