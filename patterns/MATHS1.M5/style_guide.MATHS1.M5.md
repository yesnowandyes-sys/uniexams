### Geometry Question Style Guide (MATHS1.M5)

#### 1.  Structural Typology
*   **Embedded Geometric Constraints:**
    Questions often present a diagram (e.g. square, hexagon) and impose a construction (e.g. "A circle is drawn with centre...", "extended to T such that..."). The student must bridge the properties of the original shape with the properties of the constructed element.
    *   *Example:* Q2 (Polygon with extended side), Q3 (Circle inside square).
*   **"Clockwork" Bearings:**
    Bearings questions consistently rely on North-South vertical alignment to form right-angled triangles.
    *   *Example:* Q1, Q6. The "due North/South/East/West" phrasing is the standard trigger for Pythagoras or SOHCAHTOA application in a bearing context.
*   **Deconstructed Area Problems:**
    Area questions are rarely direct applications of formulas. They require calculating a "composite" area by subtracting a standard shape (triangle/sector) from a bounding shape (rectangle/semicircle).
    *   *Example:* Q4 (Sector minus triangle), Q8 (Semicircle minus square corner), Q27 (Segment).

#### 2.  Difficulty Calibration
*   **Level 1 (Low):**
    *   Direct application of SOHCAHTOA in right-angled triangles.
    *   Calculating one dimension of a shape given others (e.g. Q11 Volume = Surface Area).
    *   Clock angles with simple fractions (Q38).
*   **Level 2 (Medium):**
    *   Properties of polygons (Interior/ exterior angles) combined with isosceles triangle properties (Q13, Q2).
    *   Circle Theorems (Tangents, Chords) requiring multi-step angle chasing (Q14).
    *   Optimization or scaling (Q19, Q30).
*   **Level 3 (High):**
    *   Coordinate geometry of circles inscribed in polygons (Q3).
    *   Algebraic Geometry involving simultaneous equations derived from geometric constraints (Q15, Q23).
    *   Transformation of functions involving reflections and translations (Q5).

#### 3.  Wording & Calculator-Free Arithmetic
*   **Format:**
    *   "What is the length/area/volume...?" is standard.
    *   "Which of the following is an expression...?" is used when the answer involves $\pi$ or surds ($\sqrt{3}$, $\sqrt{5}$).
*   **Arithmetic Patterns:**
    *   **$\pi$ Cancellation:** In Q21, the density is $0.1$ and $\pi$ is approximated as $22/7$ specifically to allow division by $7$ to result in an integer, effectively making the question calculator-free despite the presence of circles.
    *   **Special Triangles:** Geometry questions heavily favor 30-60-90 and 45-45-90 triangles.
        *   *Evidence:* Q1 (Bearings yielding $\sqrt{3}$), Q4 (Tangents yielding $\sqrt{3}$), Q9 (Angle $\pi/6$).
    *   **Integer Constrained Dimensions:** Questions are designed so that Pythagorean triples or surds simplify neatly (e.g. "radius $\sqrt{77}$").

#### 4.  Diagram Dependency
*   "Not to scale" is a standard warning.
*   Diagrams often omit labels that are described in the text (e.g. "Points X, Y, Z...").
*   **3D to 2D Projection:** 3D shapes (Q37, Q47) require the student to mentally flatten or unfold the shape (e.g. looking at the net or using the Pythagorean theorem in 3D) to find the required distance or angle.
