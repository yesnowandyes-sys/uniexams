# ESAT Biology (B4) Style Guide

## Question Structure Patterns
1.  **Multi-Stage Probability Chains (Complexity 4-6)**
    *   **Format:** Pedigree Diagram + Text Description + Specific Target Probability.
    *   **Example:** Q20, Q23, Q37.
    *   **Pattern:** "Individual X mates with Individual Y (who has a Z% chance of being a carrier). What is the probability their child is affected/unaffected?"
    *   **Requirement:** Students must calculate conditional probabilities sequentially (e.g., $P(\text{child affected}) = P(\text{mother carrier}) \times P(\text{father passes}) \times P(\text{child inherits recessive})$).

2.  **Abstract/Diagram-Based Logic (Complexity 3-5)**
    *   **Format:** Diagram of chromosomes, cells, or mutation tables without context.
    *   **Example:** Q8, Q13, Q30.
    *   **Pattern:** A table of codons/amino acids is provided. A specific mutation is described. Students must deduce the effect (silent/missense/nonsense) and apply it to a phenotypic outcome.
    *   **Constraint:** Students are not expected to memorize the genetic code; all necessary translation keys are provided in the question.

3.  **Population Mathematics (Complexity 5-7)**
    *   **Format:** Word problem with large numbers (populations), percentages, and allele counts.
    *   **Example:** Q5, Q28, Q32.
    *   **Pattern:** "In a population of X... Y are affected, Z are carriers. How many alleles of type A exist?"
    *   **Arithmetic:** Requires careful handling of large numbers and the factor of 2 for diploidy. Often involves $2 \times (\text{Homozygous}) + 1 \times (\text{Heterozygous})$.

4.  **Statement Verification (Multi-Truth)**
    *   **Format:** 3 statements about evolution/genetics. Select the combination (1/2/3/1&2/etc).
    *   **Example:** Q4, Q9, Q21.
    *   **Pattern:** Tests precise definitions of "Selection Pressure," "Mutation," "Adaptation," and "Dominance."

## Difficulty Calibration
*   **Band 1-3 (Easy):** Basic terminology (Allele, Gene, Chromosome), single-generation Monohybrid crosses (Aa x Aa), identifying genotypes from simple phenotypes.
*   **Band 4-6 (Medium):** Multi-generational pedigrees, sex-linkage (X-chromosome logic), carrier probability calculations, Hardy-Weinberg application (basic allele counting), Codon table reading.
*   **Band 7-9 (Hard):** Simultaneous inheritance of two traits (Dihybrid logic but often separated), conditional probability (e.g., "Given they are a girl, what is the chance..."), lethal allele ratio adjustments, distinguishing between Lamarckian and Darwinian evolution in complex scenarios.

## Wording Conventions
*   **"Unknown Genotype":** A key phrase (Q1, Q18) signaling the need for a test cross or probability assessment.
*   **"Autosomal":** Explicitly stated to rule out sex-linkage confusion.
*   **"Random Mating / Random Process":** Used in population questions to justify the use of probability/ratios.
*   **"Assume no new mutations":** Standard disclaimer to isolate inheritance patterns.

## Calculator-Free Arithmetic Patterns
*   **Fractions over Decimals:** Probabilities are usually expressed as fractions (1/4, 1/16) in the options.
*   **Large Number Estimation:** Population questions often use numbers like 58,000 or 1,600.
    *   *Trick:* The answer is often a simple doubling (e.g., 29 albinos -> 58 alleles) or requires summing $2 \times \text{Group}_1 + 1 \times \text{Group}_2$.
*   **Ratio Simplification:** Students must recognize that 0.25 is equivalent to 1:3 or 25%.
