## 1. Executive Summary

The ESAT (Engineering and Science Admissions Test) is a computer-based admissions test used by Imperial College London and Cambridge University for engineering and science courses. It replaced the NSAA and ENGAA starting in 2024. The test consists of multiple-choice questions across five modules: Mathematics 1 (compulsory), Mathematics 2, Biology, Chemistry, and Physics. Each module contains 27 questions to be completed in 40 minutes. Scoring uses a 1.0–9.0 scale with no negative marking.

## 3. Module Structure

Students take **3 modules total**:
- **Mathematics 1** — compulsory for all candidates
- **2 additional modules** — chosen based on course requirements

### Module Combinations by Course Type

| Course Type | Module Combination |
|---|---|
| **Engineering** | Mathematics 1 + Mathematics 2 + Physics |
| **Physics/Engineering** | Mathematics 1 + Mathematics 2 + Physics |
| **Chemistry/Chemical Engineering** | Mathematics 1 + Chemistry + (Maths 2 or Physics) |
| **Life Sciences/Natural Sciences** | Mathematics 1 + Chemistry + Biology |
| **Biomedical** | Mathematics 1 + Biology + Chemistry |

> **Note:** Specific module requirements vary by institution and course. Candidates should verify which modules their target course requires.

| **Question type** | Multiple-choice (5 options: A–E) |

- ~1.5 minutes per question average
- Questions are designed to be challenging within the time limit
- Strategic skipping of hardest questions is advisable

## 5. Scoring System

| Property | Details |
|---|---|
| **Score scale** | 1.0 to 9.0 (reported to one decimal place) |
| **Negative marking** | None — no penalty for wrong answers |
| **Scoring method** | Raw marks converted to scaled score |
| **Per-module scoring** | Each module receives its own independent score |
| **Typical competitive scores** | 6.0–8.0+ for top engineering/science courses |
| **Guessing strategy** | Always answer every question (no penalty) |

### Score Interpretation
- **9.0**: Exceptional — virtually all correct
- **7.0–8.0**: Strong — competitive for top courses
- **5.0–6.0**: Moderate — may be sufficient for some courses
- **Below 5.0**: Below competitive threshold

## 7. Question Types

All ESAT questions are **multiple-choice with 5 options (A–E)**. Question styles include:

| Question Style | Description | Example Pattern |
|---|---|---|
| **Direct calculation** | Solve for a numerical value | "Calculate the velocity after 3 seconds..." |
| **Formula application** | Apply a formula to find an unknown | "What is the resistance of the circuit?" |
| **Conceptual/definition** | Identify correct statement or principle | "Which statement about enzyme activity is correct?" |
| **Graphical/data interpretation** | Read and analyze charts/graphs | "From the graph, determine the half-life..." |
| **Multi-step reasoning** | Combine 2–3 concepts to solve | "Calculate the final temperature considering energy losses..." |
| **Algebraic manipulation** | Rearrange and solve equations | "Express x in terms of a and b..." |
| **Diagram-based** | Interpret circuit diagrams, force diagrams | "In the circuit shown, what is the current through R1?" |
| **Error/exception identification** | Find which statement is incorrect | "Which of these statements about ionic bonding is NOT true?" |

### Question Difficulty Distribution
- **Easy (GCSE/early AS level)**: ~20% of questions — straightforward application
- **Medium (AS/A-Level)**: ~50% — require multi-step thinking
- **Hard (A-Level/Above)**: ~30% — novel scenarios, complex multi-step problems

## 8. Representative Sample Questions

### Sample 1: Mathematics 1 — Compound Interest

> A sum of £5000 is invested at 4% compound interest per annum. What is the value after 3 years to the nearest pound?
>
> A) £5,600  
> B) £5,624  
> C) £5,648  
> D) £5,672  
> E) £5,700

**Answer:** B) £5,624  
**Working:** 5000 × (1.04)³ = 5000 × 1.124864 = £5,624.32 ≈ £5,624

---

### Sample 2: Mathematics 2 — Calculus

> Find the gradient of the curve y = 3x² − 4x + 1 at the point where x = 2.
>
> A) 4  
> B) 5  
> C) 8  
> D) 12  
> E) 16

**Answer:** C) 8  
**Working:** dy/dx = 6x − 4. At x = 2: 6(2) − 4 = 8

---

### Sample 3: Physics — Mechanics

> A ball is thrown vertically upward at 15 m/s. Taking g = 10 m/s², what is the maximum height reached?
>
> A) 7.5 m  
> B) 10.0 m  
> C) 11.25 m  
> D) 15.0 m  
> E) 22.5 m

**Answer:** C) 11.25 m  
**Working:** v² = u² − 2as → 0 = 225 − 20s → s = 11.25 m

---

### Sample 4: Chemistry — Moles

> How many moles are there in 44.8 litres of CO₂ at standard temperature and pressure (STP)? (Molar volume at STP = 22.4 L/mol)
>
> A) 0.5 mol  
> B) 1.0 mol  
> C) 1.5 mol  
> D) 2.0 mol  
> E) 2.5 mol

**Answer:** D) 2.0 mol  
**Working:** 44.8 / 22.4 = 2.0 mol

---

### Sample 5: Biology — Cell Division

> During which phase of mitosis do sister chromatids separate and move to opposite poles?
>
> A) Prophase  
> B) Metaphase  
> C) Anaphase  
> D) Telophase  
> E) Cytokinesis

**Answer:** C) Anaphase  
**Working:** During anaphase, sister chromatids are pulled apart by spindle fibers toward opposite poles.

## 9. Difficulty Calibration

The ESAT is designed to **differentiate among high-achieving candidates**. Key difficulty factors:

1. **Time pressure** — 40 minutes for 27 questions (89 seconds/question) with no calculator
2. **No calculator** — mental arithmetic and estimation skills required
3. **Multi-step problems** — questions often require 3–5 calculation steps
4. **Novel contexts** — physics/chemistry problems use unfamiliar scenarios
5. **Application over recall** — emphasis on applying knowledge, not memorizing facts
6. **Trap distractors** — wrong answer options are designed to catch common errors

### Implications for Question Generation Engine
- Generate questions with **realistic multi-step paths**
- Include **plausible distractors** based on common student errors
- Calibrate difficulty to match the ~1.5 min/question time budget
- Ensure **no calculator** constraint is reflected in numerical complexity
- Distribute difficulty: 20% easy, 50% medium, 30% hard

## 11. Key Takeaways for Question Generation Engine

1. **Format**: 5-option multiple choice, always
2. **Structure**: 27 questions per module, 40-minute time limit per module
3. **Core module**: Mathematics 1 is always required
4. **Subject depth**: A-Level standard knowledge (not beyond), but applied in novel ways
5. **No calculator**: All calculations must be solvable mentally or with simple working
6. **Plausible distractors**: Wrong answers should reflect common misconceptions
7. **Difficulty mix**: Aim for 20% easy / 50% medium / 30% hard distribution
8. **Content spec**: Follow the official ESAT Content Specification for topic coverage
9. **Scoring**: 1.0–9.0 scale, no negative marking — students should attempt every question
10. **Predecessor papers**: ENGAA (2016–2023) and NSAA (2016–2023) provide the best source of validated questions for pattern analysis
